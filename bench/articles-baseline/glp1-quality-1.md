# Beyond the Scale: What GLP-1 Drugs Have Shown for the Heart and Kidneys, and What Is Still Untested for the Brain

## The Heart: Semaglutide in People Who Already Have Cardiovascular Disease

The SELECT trial enrolled 17,604 adults with overweight or obesity and established cardiovascular disease, but no diabetes. Each received once-weekly semaglutide or placebo. Semaglutide cut the primary endpoint, cardiovascular death, non-fatal heart attack, or non-fatal stroke, by 20% in relative terms. Events hit 6.5% of the semaglutide group and 8.0% of placebo over a mean 39.8 months, an absolute gap of 1.5 percentage points. [1]

This is secondary-prevention evidence. It does not show that people without established cardiovascular disease would gain the same benefit, or that each part of the composite endpoint improved on its own.

SELECT also cannot show the benefit was independent of weight loss, which changes blood pressure, blood sugar, and other risk factors, or separate those pathways from any direct effect on the heart. Reviews describe the cardiovascular benefits of GLP-1 receptor agonists as well-supported. [2, 3] That support is strongest where a trial tested a named drug in a defined group, not for every GLP-1 drug in every patient.

## The Kidneys: A Dedicated Trial, Tested as an Add-On

FLOW began in 2019 and enrolled 3,533 adults with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease across 28 countries and roughly 400 sites. Participants received once-weekly semaglutide 1.0 mg or placebo alongside standard care, so the trial cannot show semaglutide replacing existing kidney-protective treatment. [4]

The material provided gives no treatment effect, no event rates, and no results for the separate components of the composite primary endpoint. [4] On this evidence alone, the claim that semaglutide slows kidney disease in FLOW cannot be checked.

A 2026 consensus report says GLP-1-based therapies offer organ protection and improve long-term outcomes in type 2 diabetes, and it advocates earlier initiation, potentially from diagnosis. [5] That is a recommendation, not a trial result. The report covers GLP-1 receptor agonists and tirzepatide, a dual GLP-1/GIP agonist. [5] A benefit seen with a dual agonist cannot be credited to GLP-1 activity alone or extended to every GLP-1 receptor agonist. And its claim that benefits are largely independent of glucose lowering does not mean independent of weight loss. [5]

## The Liver and Inflammation: A Direct Effect?

A 2024 phase 2 randomized trial examined survodutide in metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis, or MASH, and fibrosis. [2] The material provided reports no result. It says nothing about whether liver histology improved, or about cirrhosis, liver failure, or death. The trial adds a research question, not evidence of liver protection.

One source says GLP-1 receptor agonists may have anti-inflammatory effects beyond what weight loss alone would predict. [6] It gives no inflammatory endpoints and no method for separating a direct effect from weight loss. GLP-1 receptors are found throughout the body, but that does not prove a clinically useful effect in any organ. [7]

## The Brain: An Emerging Frontier

Neurology and psychiatry are unsettled. Research is investigating GLP-1 receptor agonists for stroke, addiction, and Parkinson's disease, with other work on vascular events and movement disorders. [8] The material provided reports no results. A list of conditions under investigation is not evidence of brain protection.

## The Limits and the Future

Potential complications must be understood and actively managed. [3] These sources do not name them or give their rates, so benefits cannot be weighed against harms from this material alone. Tolerability and stopping treatment also affect whether a trial benefit reaches a patient.

These drugs are one part of treatment for type 2 diabetes or obesity, not the whole plan. [9] One source says they often surpass lifestyle interventions alone for weight loss and metabolic and cardiovascular health, but it does not define the lifestyle program or show the comparison held equally across those outcomes. [10]
